Best Voice-to-Text Apps for Mac in 2026: A Complete Comparison

We tested every serious Mac dictation app so you don't have to. Here's how Wspr, Wispr Flow, Superwhisper, MacWhisper, and Apple Dictation compare on price, privacy, accuracy, and features.

Voice-to-text has matured significantly across desktop platforms. GPU acceleration made on-device speech recognition practical, and OpenAI's Whisper models gave third-party developers a world-class transcription engine to build on. The result: several compelling dictation apps that go far beyond what built-in dictation offers.

But they differ wildly in pricing, privacy, and approach. Some send your audio to the cloud. Some charge monthly subscriptions. Some only transcribe files, not live speech. We broke down the five most popular options to help you pick the right one.

1. Wspr — Best Overall Value

Wspr is a cross-platform app that uses whisper.cpp with GPU acceleration to run OpenAI's Whisper models directly on your device. Press a global hotkey, speak, and your transcription is pasted into whatever app you're using. It also handles file transcription for audio and video files. Available on macOS, Windows, and Linux.

What sets Wspr apart is the combination of on-device privacy, cross-platform support, AI rewriting flexibility, and price. It supports AI rewriting via OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google Gemini using your own API key. And the whole thing costs $14.99, once, forever.

Pros:

  • $14.99 one-time — no subscription, no recurring costs
  • 100% on-device transcription via whisper.cpp with GPU acceleration
  • Cross-platform: macOS, Windows, and Linux
  • AI rewriting with OpenAI, Anthropic, or Gemini using your own API key
  • Both live dictation and file transcription
  • 99 languages with automatic detection
  • Free tier with 50 transcriptions to try before buying

Cons:

  • Newer app, smaller community

Download Wspr free or get Wspr Pro for $14.99.

2. Wispr Flow — Best Cloud-Based Option

Wispr Flow takes a fundamentally different approach: it sends your audio to the cloud for transcription and rewriting. The upside is broad platform coverage — Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android — and a proprietary AI trained specifically for dictation context. The downside is that every word you speak leaves your device.

Pros:

  • Polished UI and smooth dictation experience
  • Context-aware AI that adapts to what you're writing
  • Native apps on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android
  • Free tier with 2,000 words/week on Mac and Windows
  • 100+ languages with automatic detection

Cons:

  • $15/month on monthly billing, or $12/month billed annually ($144–$180/year) on the Pro plan
  • All audio is sent to cloud servers for processing — no offline option
  • Proprietary AI — no choice of provider
  • Requires internet connection to function
  • No Linux support

3. Superwhisper — Strong On-Device Option

Superwhisper runs Whisper models locally and is one of the more mature Mac-first dictation apps. It supports both live system-wide dictation and file transcription, and offers a broad menu of AI providers for text rewriting.

Pros:

  • On-device transcription with Whisper models — works offline
  • Clean interface with system-wide dictation
  • Supports audio/video file transcription on the Pro tier
  • AI rewriting via multiple providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Llama, and others) with your own API key
  • Meeting recording and transcription built in
  • Available on macOS, Windows, and iOS
  • 100+ languages

Cons:

  • Subscription pricing (a Lifetime tier is available but costs more up front)
  • No Linux support
  • Free tier is limited — Pro features require payment after the trial

4. MacWhisper — Best for File Transcription

MacWhisper is one of the most established Whisper-powered apps on Mac. It started as a file-transcription tool and has since added system-wide dictation; file transcription is still where it shines, with batch processing, subtitle export, and OpenAI integration for text cleanup.

Pros:

  • One-time purchase (around $29 on Gumroad, up to ~$79 for Pro/lifetime on the Mac App Store)
  • Excellent file transcription with batch processing
  • Subtitle/SRT export
  • On-device transcription — audio never leaves your Mac
  • System-wide dictation available

Cons:

  • macOS only — no Windows or Linux build
  • Live-dictation experience is secondary to the file-transcription focus
  • AI features lean on OpenAI

5. Apple Dictation — Free but Limited

Apple's built-in dictation is free and works out of the box. Press the microphone key or double-tap the Fn key, and macOS transcribes your speech. For casual use, it's fine. For anything more demanding, its limitations show quickly.

Pros:

  • Free, built into macOS
  • No setup required
  • Supports ~60 languages

Cons:

  • Noticeably lower accuracy than Whisper-based apps
  • No AI text rewriting
  • No file transcription
  • May send audio to Apple's servers (depending on settings)
  • Limited customization

The Verdict

If privacy, Linux support, and a flat one-time price matter to you, Wspr is the most compelling option. It gives you on-device transcription, AI rewriting with your choice of OpenAI, Anthropic, or Gemini using your own API key, both live and file transcription, and support for macOS, Windows, and Linux — all for $14.99 once. That's less than a single month of Wispr Flow Pro.

Wspr is the only app in this comparison with native Linux support, which makes it the obvious choice if you work on Linux or across Linux and another OS. Wispr Flow is worth considering if you want cloud-based contextual AI and dictation across phones and desktops, and you don't mind the subscription or the cloud privacy tradeoff. Superwhisper is a strong pick on Mac, Windows, or iOS if you want a mature on-device app with a broad AI provider menu and you're okay with their subscription or Lifetime pricing. MacWhisper is the right choice if your primary need is file transcription with batch processing and subtitle export. And Apple Dictation is there if you just need something basic for free.

For users who want privacy, cross-platform coverage that includes Linux, and a one-time purchase with AI polish, Wspr is the pick.
