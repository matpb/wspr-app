Best Voice-to-Text Apps for Mac in 2026: A Complete Comparison

We tested every serious Mac dictation app so you don't have to. Here's how Wspr, Wispr Flow, Superwhisper, MacWhisper, and Apple Dictation compare on price, privacy, accuracy, and features.

Voice-to-text has matured significantly across desktop platforms. GPU acceleration made on-device speech recognition practical, and OpenAI's Whisper models gave third-party developers a world-class transcription engine to build on. The result: several compelling dictation apps that go far beyond what built-in dictation offers.

But they differ wildly in pricing, privacy, and approach. Some send your audio to the cloud. Some charge monthly subscriptions. Some only transcribe files, not live speech. We broke down the five most popular options to help you pick the right one.

1. Wspr — Best Overall Value

Wspr is a cross-platform app that uses whisper.cpp with GPU acceleration to run OpenAI's Whisper models directly on your device. Press a global hotkey, speak, and your transcription is pasted into whatever app you're using. It also handles file transcription for audio and video files. Available on macOS, Windows, and Linux.

What sets Wspr apart is the combination of on-device privacy, cross-platform support, AI rewriting flexibility, and price. It supports AI rewriting via OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google Gemini using your own API key. And the whole thing costs $14.99, once, forever.

Pros:

  • $14.99 one-time — no subscription, no recurring costs
  • 100% on-device transcription via whisper.cpp with GPU acceleration
  • Cross-platform: macOS (Apple Silicon), Windows, and Linux
  • AI rewriting with OpenAI, Anthropic, or Gemini using your own API key
  • Both live dictation and file transcription
  • 99 languages with automatic detection
  • Free tier with 50 transcriptions to try before buying

Cons:

  • Newer app, smaller community

Download Wspr free or get Wspr Pro for $14.99.

2. Wispr Flow — Best Cloud-Based Option

Wispr Flow takes a fundamentally different approach: it sends your audio to the cloud for transcription and rewriting. The upside is that it works on any Mac regardless of processor, and its proprietary AI is trained specifically for dictation context. The downside is that every word you speak leaves your device.

Pros:

  • Polished UI and smooth dictation experience
  • Context-aware AI that adapts to what you're writing
  • Works on Apple Silicon Macs

Cons:

  • $15/month ($180/year) subscription
  • All audio is sent to cloud servers for processing
  • No file transcription
  • Proprietary AI — no choice of provider
  • Requires internet connection to function

3. Superwhisper — Strong On-Device Option

Superwhisper runs Whisper models locally and offers live dictation with AI rewriting through OpenAI's API. It's a solid choice if you want on-device transcription with cloud-based text polishing.

Pros:

  • On-device transcription with Whisper models
  • Clean interface with system-wide dictation
  • AI rewriting via OpenAI

Cons:

  • ~$10/month subscription ($120/year)
  • Only supports OpenAI for rewriting (no Anthropic, no Gemini)
  • No file transcription

4. MacWhisper — Best for File Transcription Only

MacWhisper focuses on transcribing audio and video files rather than live dictation. It runs Whisper models locally and offers batch processing, subtitle export, and OpenAI integration for text cleanup. If you mainly need to transcribe recordings, interviews, or meetings, it's a capable tool.

Pros:

  • One-time purchase ($30 for Standard, $80 for Pro)
  • Excellent file transcription with batch processing
  • Subtitle/SRT export
  • On-device transcription

Cons:

  • No live dictation — can't use it to type by voice
  • Pro tier costs $80
  • Only supports OpenAI for AI features

5. Apple Dictation — Free but Limited

Apple's built-in dictation is free and works out of the box. Press the microphone key or double-tap the Fn key, and macOS transcribes your speech. For casual use, it's fine. For anything more demanding, its limitations show quickly.

Pros:

  • Free, built into macOS
  • No setup required
  • Supports ~60 languages

Cons:

  • Noticeably lower accuracy than Whisper-based apps
  • No AI text rewriting
  • No file transcription
  • May send audio to Apple's servers (depending on settings)
  • Limited customization

The Verdict

If privacy and value matter to you, Wspr is the clear winner. It gives you on-device transcription, AI rewriting with your choice of OpenAI, Anthropic, or Gemini, both live and file transcription, cross-platform support for macOS, Windows, and Linux, and 99-language support — all for $14.99 once. That's less than a single month of Wispr Flow.

Wspr is also the only app in this comparison that runs on Windows and Linux, making it the clear choice if you work across platforms. Wispr Flow is worth considering if you specifically want cloud-based contextual AI and don't mind the subscription or privacy tradeoff. MacWhisper is the right choice if you only need file transcription and never plan to dictate live. And Apple Dictation is there if you just need something basic for free.

But for most users who want to turn voice into text — privately, affordably, and with AI polish — Wspr gets the job done better than anything else available in 2026.
